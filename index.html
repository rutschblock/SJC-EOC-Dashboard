Senior Leader Decisions · Reassess Within 24 Hours

3 decision areas · Demo
1. Disaster declaration determination Decision / reassessment

Determine whether incident impacts, unmet needs, local capabilities and anticipated costs warrant a local disaster declaration or escalation for state support. Record the authorized decision-maker, rationale, time and next review. Declaration is not automatic.

Decision prompt: Are local capabilities exceeded or likely to be exceeded, and what support or authorities are needed?

2. First responder needs and operational sustainability Priority support

Confirm unmet needs with the IC and response agencies: personnel relief, EMS coverage, responder safety, communications, access, equipment, staging and mutual aid. Identify which shortfalls require EOC coordination without directing field tactics.

Decision prompt: What must leadership authorize or elevate to sustain safe response operations through the next operational period?

3. Incident cost burn rate and funding outlook Financial visibility

Obtain a concise estimate from Finance/Administration: actual expenditures to date, current daily burn rate, projected costs over the next 24–72 hours, and potential funding or spending-authority constraints. Flag significant uncertainty and commitments requiring leadership decisions.

Decision prompt: At the current burn rate, when might existing spending authority or available funding become insufficient?

Leadership summary only. Detailed expense tracking, procurement and reimbursement remain in the established finance workflow.

Senior leader reassessment: Review each decision area in less than 24 hours, or immediately when responder needs, community impacts, or cost forecasts materially change. Record decisions and escalation through established channels.

Priority: Life Saving · Goal: Address Community Impacts. A compact decision framework, not a second lifelines feed.

Lifeline Stabilization Problem Frame

What is disrupted? Who is affected? What dependencies threaten additional services? What would stabilize the community?

Community Impact · How disruption affects survivors
When a lifeline is affected by a disaster, survivors may experience disruptions which reduce their ability to receive critical services and recover from the effects of the incident. These lifeline impacts are assessed and addressed based on overall impact to the community, otherwise known as Community Impact.

Use the community consequence—not merely the status of an asset—to prioritize stabilization and recovery.

Early Recovery & Private-Sector Flow

Begin restoration planning during response. Prioritize restoring private-sector capacity before assuming government commodity distribution is necessary.

Transportation and access restoration

CDOT coordination, route status, safe access, repair or reopening milestones, essential delivery requirements.

Food, fuel and essential commodities

Contact operators; assess inventories, delivery schedules, staff availability and ability to resupply.

Power and communications dependencies

Identify how outages affect fuel pumps, payments, refrigeration, medical access and logistics.

Re-entry and regulatory issues

Identify potential credentialing, permits, escorts or waivers; refer decisions to the proper authority.

Government support threshold

Identify service gaps that private-sector restoration cannot address, then assess targeted government support or points of distribution.
